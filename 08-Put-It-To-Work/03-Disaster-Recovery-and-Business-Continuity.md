# 🔄 Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity  

---

# 🛡 Why This Matters

Even with strong security controls:

✔ Incidents can still happen  
✔ Breaches can still occur  
✔ Systems can still fail  

Security professionals must prepare for:

- Financial loss  
- Operational downtime  
- Reputational damage  
- Customer trust erosion  

Preparation is not optional — it is strategic.

---

# 🧠 The 4-Step Security Process

Before business continuity and disaster recovery plans are created, security teams follow this sequence:

---

## 1️⃣ Identify Assets

    What must we protect?

Examples:
- Customer data
- Financial records
- Production systems
- Intellectual property
- Manufacturing systems

---

## 2️⃣ Identify Threats

    What could damage those assets?

Examples:
- Ransomware
- Insider threats
- DDoS attacks
- Data breaches
- Hardware failure

---

## 3️⃣ Detect & Protect

    How do we detect threats?

Examples:
- SIEM systems
- Endpoint detection
- Log monitoring
- Access controls
- Encryption

---

## 4️⃣ Plan for Disruption

    What happens if prevention fails?

This is where:

- Business Continuity Plan (BCP)
- Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

are created.

---

# 📘 Business Continuity Plan (BCP)

## 🔍 Definition

A Business Continuity Plan outlines procedures to keep business operations running during and after disruption.

Goal:
Minimize operational impact.

---

# 🧩 4 Essential BCP Steps

---

## 1️⃣ Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

    What happens if systems go down?

Analyze:
- Financial losses
- Customer impact
- Legal impact
- Downtime cost

---

## 2️⃣ Recovery of Critical Functions

    How do we restore essential operations?

Identify:
- Core business processes
- Required staff
- Backup workflows
- Alternative systems

---

## 3️⃣ Form a Continuity Team

BCP Team typically includes:

- Cybersecurity
- IT
- HR
- Communications
- Operations
- Executive leadership

Security is collaborative.

---

## 4️⃣ Conduct Training

    Practice before disaster strikes.

- Tabletop exercises
- Simulated attacks
- Ransomware drills
- Communication simulations

Prepared teams reduce chaos.

---

# 🔥 Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

## 🔍 Definition

A Disaster Recovery Plan outlines technical steps to restore systems and data after an incident.

Goal:
Restore infrastructure and eliminate the threat.

---

# 🛠 DRP Core Components

---

## 1️⃣ Restore Software

Examples:
- Reinstall systems
- Restore backups
- Patch vulnerabilities
- Validate integrity

---

## 2️⃣ Restore Hardware

Examples:
- Replace compromised servers
- Restore network devices
- Verify infrastructure health

---

## 3️⃣ Identify Impacted Data & Applications

    What was affected?

- Corrupted databases
- Lost files
- Compromised applications
- Interrupted services

---

# 🆚 BCP vs DRP (Clear Difference)

| Business Continuity | Disaster Recovery |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Keeps business running | Restores systems |
| Operational focus | Technical focus |
| People + process | Systems + infrastructure |
| During & after disruption | After disruption |

They work together — not separately.

---

# 🏢 Real-World Scenario

🚨 Ransomware hits manufacturing servers.

Without planning:
- Production stops
- Customers lose trust
- Financial loss escalates

With BCP + DRP:
- Backup systems activate
- IT restores encrypted data
- Operations resume quickly
- Damage minimized

Preparation reduces impact.

---

# 🧠 Security Mindset Application

As a security analyst, ask:

- What is the worst-case scenario?
- What systems are mission-critical?
- How fast must we recover? (RTO)
- How much data can we lose? (RPO)
- Who is responsible for execution?

Thinking ahead = professional maturity.
