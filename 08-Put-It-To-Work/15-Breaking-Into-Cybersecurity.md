# 👤 The Cover Letter: Your Story, Not Your Resume

> “A resume tells me what you've done.  
> A cover letter tells me who you are.” – Garvey, Global Staffing Manager

## 🧠 Why Cybersecurity?

Hiring managers want to know:

- Why this field?
- Why now?
- Why this company?
- What brought you here?

Most strong candidates have a reason:
- Victim of cybercrime
- Inspired by protecting others
- Career pivot toward purpose
- Passion for solving complex problems

Tell that story.

---

# ✍️ Writing a Strong Cover Letter

## 📏 Length
No strict formula — but:

- Start with a short personal intro
- Then quickly focus on what makes you unique
- Keep it concise and impactful

---

## 💡 What To Include

### 1️⃣ Who You Are
- Background
- Personal motivation
- Brief life context

### 2️⃣ Why Cybersecurity?
- What excites you?
- What problem do you want to solve?
- What didn’t you find in your previous career?

### 3️⃣ Overcoming Adversity
- A challenge you faced
- How you handled ambiguity
- How you'll handle it in this role

### 4️⃣ Soft Skills
- Collaboration
- Communication
- Leadership
- Curiosity
- Resilience

---

## 🚫 Do NOT Standardize It

Tailor your cover letter to:

- Company mission
- Their products
- Their purpose
- Their values

Make their mission part of your story.

# 🧠 Technical Interview Preparation

## 🎯 What Interviewers Actually Want

They are NOT testing:

- Speed
- Memorization
- Trivia knowledge

They ARE testing:

✔ Do you understand fundamentals?  
✔ Can you explain them clearly?  
✔ Can you think through ambiguity?  
✔ Can you ask clarifying questions?  

---

# 🛠 Tools To Know (Entry-Level)

## 🔍 SIEM Tools
- Splunk
- Basic log analysis concepts
- Alert triage
- Why SIEM exists

## 📡 Network Tools
- Wireshark
- Packet inspection basics
- TCP/IP understanding

## 🧩 Fundamentals

- Network security
- Web application security
- Operating system internals
- Security protocols (TLS, HTTPS, SSH)
- NIST frameworks (CSF, 800-53 basics)

If these tools didn’t exist:
> How would you solve the problem manually?

That’s deeper understanding.

---

# 🧠 Answering Open-Ended Questions

Open-ended questions are:

- Ambiguous by design
- Complex by design

### Step 1: Ask Clarifying Questions
Never dive in blindly.

Example:
- What environment?
- What constraints?
- What priority?
- What threat model?

---

### Step 2: Use the STAR Method

**S – Situation**  
**T – Task**  
**A – Action**  
**R – Result**

Keeps your answer structured and easy to follow.

---

### Step 3: Think Out Loud

Thinking out loud helps interviewers:

- See your reasoning
- See your approach
- Help guide you if needed

Even if you don’t finish perfectly, being on the right track matters.

---

# 🧑‍💻 If You Don’t Know the Answer

Say:

> “I don’t know, but here’s how I would approach it.”

This shows:

✔ Honesty  
✔ Analytical thinking  
✔ Growth mindset  

Never guess blindly. Never lie.
